Welcome to the revived SkooBot project!

Given that I've decided to move forward with this with heavy AI assistance, I wanted to make sure that there was a verifiable note here that is written directly from a human. That is also why this is a .md file with that is absolutely devoid of any markdown formatting...it is being written lazily by an actual human rushing to get a pre-release build ready for consumption.

First, I want to thank everyone who has ever used or given feedback on SkooBot. While I have not been actively maintaining it for years, I do occasionally think about it and searching references in the Discord has always yielded people having positive and interesting discussions about SkooBot. I made SkooBot chiefly for myself, but I did decide to share it becaue I hoped that I had made something useful enough that others would appreciate that and be able to benefit from my work. It makes me happy to see even a few people benefitting from that effort.

Another purpose the SkooBot project served was to give me a chance to learn/try GitHub for managing a project. I was already familiar with Git but I felt that GitHub had some benefits for organization, and it actually genuinely helped direct the development sprints that I did back then.

Recently, several of my friends have been showing off projects they've put together with AI coding tools. Friends who know little or nothing about programming, making large, elaborate, complex solutions to problems they've encountered or ideas they've had. What they were able to come up with was nothing short of amazing. I've kept AI tools relatively at arms length over the past ~4 years. I felt they were inelegant, inefficient, and you'd be lucky to get something that works, and God help you if you look under the hood at whatever eldritch monstrosity it came up with to cobble together something that cosmetically met the specs. Increasingly, lately, I've had this thought that I'm maybe being to closed-minded about the potential. So this project serves the extra purpose of giving me the chance to learn just what strengths and weaknesses that vibe-coding has.

For the record, this is the second such project: attempt 1 was pointing Codex at the skoobot repo and saying "go". Several hundred thousands of useless lines authorization objects, hash pinning, historical validator compatibility, reverse proofs, and preservation semantics later, I was feeling very vindicated but also like perhaps I just needed to provide a *bit* more input than simply "go". That experiment is still running in parallel to this one--it actually had some really good proposals up-front that were *very* mechanically interesting to me, and I also want to see if it's even possible to pull out of that rabbit hole it got stuck down.

This project, at the time of writing immediately prior to preparing a release build of skoobot_reclauded 0.1.0, has involved approximately 12-24 hours worth of keyboard time. Much of that time has been environment configuration and setting up accounts for the Claude VM I have this sandboxed in to be able to access github and push code. Some of it has been reading through proposed architecture options from Claude and making very high level decisions about which direction to go. A lot of it, though, has just been writing a wish-list. I download a build, I play for a bit, I identify a friction point, write a sentence or two about it, and tell Claude to track it as an issue. At the end of a session I may have half a dozen or a dozen feature requests or bug fixes to report. I tell Claude "fix them all, ask me if you need a decision". Sometimes I'm given a few multiple-choice options for proposed designs. Sometimes it consults me with something like "this might need a rework of system X, is this OK with you?" ... So far just about every task I've given it has turned out better than I could ever expect. I had it track what I thought was a long-term-goal of figuring out a way to automatically suggest talent configuration. In the immediate response, it had a complete write-up of how that could be accomplished and logged it as an issue. That night, when I told it "get any decisions from me and plan to work on all issues that are ready for implementation unattended" I didn't expect to wake up to a version of SkooBot that had a (reasonably successful but not perfect) automatic talent configuration button already in a playable build...

I guess the point of this entire ramble is mostly just to convey this: I can see how vibe-coding is justifiably a somewhat derogatory term for low-effort implementation. I've used it as such for quite a while. Now that I've actually applied it to a reasonably complex project and experienced both pitfalls and successes, I'm much more inclined to distinguish entirely between the skills necessary to successfully hand-program something vs the skills necessary to vibe-code something. I'm not going to try and convince you that "every single vibe-coded project was the product of passion, blood, sweat, and tears", but I will try to convince skeptics that like many tools, vibe-coding can be lazily applied, or it can be adeptly applied. This project is my attempt to learn how to do the latter. I hope that while I try to improve myself, my effort can be a benefit to at least a few people.
